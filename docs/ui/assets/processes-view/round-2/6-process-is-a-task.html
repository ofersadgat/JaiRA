6 · A process is a task

  • A process is a task. Its type is process and its workflow is the built-in Process. On the board that is a column among Session, Planning and Events, and its cards are task cards: title, a badge with the state (◉ listening :8145, ▶ running, ⏸ waiting for input, ✗ exited 1), the machine and workspace chip, and the time.
  • Where it came from is a fact of the task, beside the machine chip: run_in_background, this turn, left running, escaped · WMI, Mine · watched. The task that started it is its parent: the card says ↰ from Dev server and the gutter, and the parent's card counts it (↳ 6 processes · 4 running · 1 escaped · 1 exited 1).
  • Why left running and escaped are origins, not states: a state says what the process is doing now and what can come next. serve and the escaped node.exe are both listening, and they will exit or be stopped like any other. Left running and escaped say how they came to be the task's, and that never changes, so they sit with the other facts. As states they would hide that both are listening on a port.
  • The sidebar's Tasks tab lists processes as task rows in the same status groups (Running, Waiting for you, Failed, Done), with a process glyph, their Process state and ↰ from … under the title. Hover gives the three places (serve dist); the selected task's children are highlighted on the board.
  • Pressing the column's head (Process ›) goes into the workflow (board 7). Pressing a card opens the task, which is its card (board 8).
