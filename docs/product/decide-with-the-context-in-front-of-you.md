# A decision comes with what it is about, and your answer can say exactly what is wrong

> As a developer, when work asks me to decide, I can see what the decision is about, pick from what is offered or give my own answer, and point at exactly which part is wrong, so that my decisions are informed and my feedback lands where it applies.

## Without this, a decision is answered without what it concerns and feedback leaves the work guessing

The person is a developer answering the decisions their processes stop for. A
decision asked without the document or details it concerns gets answered from
memory or approved unread. Feedback that says only that something is wrong
leaves the work that revises it to guess which part.

## The decision arrives with its subject, and your answer can be your own words or tied to a passage

A decision arrives together with the document or details it concerns and with
what each offered answer means. Where the process allows it, the person can
answer in their own words when none of the offered answers fits.

Several related questions can be asked as one decision. The person can change
any answer until all of them are sent together.

Feedback on a document can be attached to the exact passage it is about, so the
work that revises the document is told where to look.

Where the process allows it, the person's answers can raise follow-up
questions, and the person answers those too before the work carries on.

## A decision made without its context becomes a rubber stamp

People shown decisions without what they concern learn to approve without
reading, and then keeping the decision protects nothing. Feedback tied to a
passage means the next version fixes what was wrong instead of guessing at it,
so a document is sent back once rather than again for the same problem.
